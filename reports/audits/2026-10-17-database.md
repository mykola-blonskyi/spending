## Scope

- Every query site in `apps/api/src`. That covers sync (push, pull, resolve, prune), ledger,
  imports (create, undo, review, confirm, learn, transfer suggestions), analytics, budgets,
  recurring, family operations and purge, data rights (export, account deletion), FX (store,
  backfill), and auth/session.
- Business rules BR-17 to BR-20, BR-28, BR-30, BR-35, BR-37 and BR-38. ADR-006, ADR-013 and
  ADR-016. `deploy/after-restore.sql` and the restore runbook in `docs/operations.md`.

## How it was measured

- Scratch database `audit_db` on `spending-postgres-1`, migrated with `pnpm db:migrate`. It was
  dropped afterwards.
- Seed. 20 users, 1 family of 4, 84 accounts (a quarter in USD), 420 categories, and 195,000
  transactions over five years. User 1's personal workspace and the family hold 50,000 rows each,
  and every other personal workspace holds 5,000. 2% of rows are pending review, 2% are transfers
  and 3% are soft-deleted.
- `drizzle-kit generate` was run against a copy of the migrations folder. The result was "No
  schema changes, nothing to migrate". `pnpm db:check` passed with 12 migrations in order, and
  `drizzle-kit check` is fine.
- `lock_timeout` was tested against `pg_advisory_xact_lock(7302001)` held by a second session.
  The waiter was cancelled with "canceling statement due to lock timeout", so the pool's 10 s
  `lock_timeout` does bound the wait for the ledger lock.
- `suggestTransfers` was benchmarked in isolation with 5,000 imported rows and 35,000 candidates
  (about 19 per day, a quarter of them in another currency). It took 197 ms.

#### L1. A family deletion rewrites transfer rows in unrelated workspaces

- **Where.** `apps/api/src/workspaces/family.service.ts:395-399`. This is the last statement of
  `purge`.

  ```sql
  update transactions t set transfer_id = gen_random_uuid(), transfer_outside = true
  where t.transfer_id is not null and not t.transfer_outside and exists (
    select 1 from transactions o
    where o.transfer_id = t.transfer_id and o.id <> t.id and o.workspace_id <> t.workspace_id)
  ```

- **The problem.** Nothing limits the statement to the family being deleted, so it scans every
  transfer row in the database.
- **How the state arises.** A sync upsert ignores deleted peers (`sync.service.ts:384` and
  `mutations.ts:221-229`). So an author can move a live side A of a half-linked transfer into a
  family while its partner B is soft-deleted in their personal workspace. ADR-016 accepts that
  half-linked state.
- **Failure scenario.** Reproduced on the scratch database.
  1. A lives in family F2 and B is deleted in a personal workspace. Both share transfer X.
  2. The owner of an unrelated family F1 deletes F1.
  3. A and B get new transfer ids, `transfer_outside = true` and a version bump. On A the version
     went from 1 to 2.
  4. The members of F2 see A change from a linked transfer to a one-sided one, though nobody in
     F2 did anything.
  5. Any offline edit of A on an F2 device now turns into a conflict (BR-19).
- **Cost.** The scan runs under the global ledger lock and grows with the number of transfers in
  the whole database. It took 10 ms at 3,900 transfers.
- **Minimal fix.** Limit the statement to the transfer ids the family held. Read them before the
  move, then filter on them.

  ```ts
  const { rows } = await tx.execute<{ id: string }>(sql`
    select distinct transfer_id as id from transactions
    where workspace_id = ${familyId} and transfer_id is not null and not transfer_outside`);
  // ...existing move...
  if (rows.length > 0)
    await tx.execute(sql`
    update transactions t set transfer_id = gen_random_uuid(), transfer_outside = true
    where t.transfer_id = any(${rows.map((r) => r.id)}::uuid[]) and not t.transfer_outside
      and exists (select 1 from transactions o where o.transfer_id = t.transfer_id
                  and o.id <> t.id and o.workspace_id <> t.workspace_id)`);
  ```

  No migration is needed. Add an integration test in which a second family's half-linked
  transfer survives the purge unchanged.

#### L2. A changed display name never reaches family devices for existing rows

- **Where.**
  - `ledger/views.ts:197` puts `author.displayName` into every pulled row. The name comes from
    the `users` join in `ledger.service.ts:82` and `:98`.
  - `users.service.ts:86-96` (`updateProfile`) and the login upsert (`users.service.ts:48-55`)
    change `users.display_name`.
  - Nothing bumps the `revision` of the rows that user authored.
- **Failure scenario.** Ann renames herself to "Anna", in the profile or at her identity
  provider. Every family device keeps showing "Ann" on her old rows
  (`apps/web/src/features/family/author-badge.tsx:33`), and "Anna" on her new ones. This lasts
  until each old row happens to be edited again. The avatar is not affected, because it is
  already read live from the members list (`author-badge.tsx:23-28`).
- **Minimal fix.** A client change, no migration. Resolve the name the same way the avatar is
  resolved. Take the `displayName` from `findFamily(workspaces).members`, and fall back to
  `entry.author.displayName` only for former members.

  Bumping the revision of every authored row on each rename would also work, but it costs a
  full re-pull for the whole family.

#### L3. The review list ignores the pending rows' own currencies when it loads rates

- **Where.** `imports/imports.service.ts:300-307` calls `transferSuggestions` with `new Map()`.
  Then `:542-544` loads quotes only for the currencies of the confirmed candidates. Compare
  `create()` (`:124-128`), which pre-loads the imported currencies.
- **Failure scenario.**
  1. A family member imports their first USD-card statement. The rows wait for review (BR-28).
  2. No confirmed USD rows exist in the date window, so USD has no quotes.
  3. In `amountGap`, `eur(row)` returns null for every USD row.
  4. The review list never suggests a cross-currency transfer between that card and the EUR
     account, which BR-38 promises.

  This is a correctness bug in a query path rather than in the schema. It is listed here because
  it lives in the candidate query that this audit was asked to check.

- **Minimal fix.** At `:542`, iterate over
  `new Set([...candidates, ...imported].map((r) => r.currency))`.

#### L4. The enum caveat from audit finding 9 is still undocumented

- **Where.** `apps/api/src/database/migrate.ts:6`. It applies all pending migrations in one
  transaction.
- **Failure scenario.** One deploy carries two migrations. The first runs
  `ALTER TYPE ... ADD VALUE`, and the second uses the new value. The deploy fails with "unsafe
  use of new value", and the site stays down (fail-closed) until a fix is deployed.
- **Minimal fix.** One line in the Rollback / Schema section of `docs/operations.md`, "Ship a new
  enum value and its first use in separate deploys." The journal-timestamp half of finding 9 is
  already enforced by `scripts/check-migrations.ts`.

### Optional hardening (no current failure path in app code)

#### H1. Only one of the four BR-37 transfer invariants is enforced in the database

| Invariant                         | Enforced by                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No category                       | CHECK `transactions_transfer_ck` (schema.ts:398-401)                                            |
| `transfer_outside` needs an id    | the same CHECK                                                                                  |
| At most two live sides            | app only, `transferError` (mutations.ts:212-230) and `transferLinks` (imports.service.ts:47-83) |
| Same workspace                    | app only, as above                                                                              |
| Opposite kinds                    | app only, as above                                                                              |
| A one-sided transfer stands alone | app only, as above                                                                              |

**Concurrency is sound.** Every writer that sets `transfer_id`, `kind`, `workspace_id` or
`deleted_at` reads the peers after taking the ledger lock. Those writers are sync upsert and
resolve, import confirm and undo, and family purge. The checks therefore never race, and the
`ledger_stamp` trigger also locks any other writer.

**The gap is direct SQL and future code paths.** One example is an operator fix with `psql`.
Another is a new endpoint that writes `transfer_id` without calling `transferError`.

If you want the database to enforce this, here is a migration sketch. It is race-free because
every writer already holds lock 7302001.

```sql
CREATE FUNCTION transaction_transfer_shape() RETURNS trigger LANGUAGE plpgsql AS $$
DECLARE n int; spaces int; kinds int; any_outside boolean;
BEGIN
  IF NEW.transfer_id IS NULL OR NEW.deleted_at IS NOT NULL THEN RETURN NULL; END IF;
  SELECT count(*), count(DISTINCT workspace_id), count(DISTINCT kind), bool_or(transfer_outside)
    INTO n, spaces, kinds, any_outside
    FROM transactions WHERE transfer_id = NEW.transfer_id AND deleted_at IS NULL;
  IF n > 2 OR spaces > 1 OR (n = 2 AND (kinds < 2 OR any_outside)) THEN
    RAISE EXCEPTION 'invalid transfer %', NEW.transfer_id USING ERRCODE = 'check_violation';
  END IF;
  RETURN NULL;
END $$;
--> statement-breakpoint
CREATE CONSTRAINT TRIGGER transactions_transfer_shape
  AFTER INSERT OR UPDATE OF transfer_id, transfer_outside, workspace_id, kind, deleted_at
  ON transactions DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED
  FOR EACH ROW EXECUTE FUNCTION transaction_transfer_shape();
```

The check is deferred to commit, so the purge's intermediate states pass. The lookup uses
`transactions_transfer_idx`, which took 0.06 ms. Before adding it, run a `SELECT` for violating
groups.

#### H2. `workspaces.owner_id` is `ON DELETE CASCADE`

- **Where.** `schema.ts:140-142` and `0000_init.sql:258`.
- **Failure scenario.** Someone runs `DELETE FROM users` directly on a family owner. The cascade
  drops the whole family workspace, including every other member's family transactions,
  accounts and categories.
- **Why there is no app path.** `DataRightsService.deleteUser` purges the family or refuses with
  `FAMILY_OWNER_MUST_TRANSFER` first, and then deletes the personal workspace before the user.
  So `RESTRICT` breaks no current flow.
- **Fix.**

  ```sql
  ALTER TABLE workspaces DROP CONSTRAINT workspaces_owner_id_users_id_fk,
    ADD CONSTRAINT workspaces_owner_id_users_id_fk FOREIGN KEY (owner_id)
    REFERENCES users(id) ON DELETE RESTRICT;
  ```

  Also change `onDelete: 'restrict'` in `schema.ts`.

#### H3. The nullable names from 0010 have no CHECK

`FamilyService.delete` compares `confirmName !== family.name` (`family.service.ts:80`), so a
family with a null name could never be deleted (BR-11). `previewInvitation` asserts
`familyName!` (`:215`). Today zod and `updateCategory` (`ledger.service.ts:187`) stop both cases.
The scratch database has 0 violating rows.

```sql
ALTER TABLE workspaces ADD CONSTRAINT workspaces_family_named_ck CHECK (kind = 'personal' OR name IS NOT NULL);
ALTER TABLE categories ADD CONSTRAINT categories_named_or_default_ck CHECK (name IS NOT NULL OR system_key IS NOT NULL);
```

#### H4. Resolved `sync_conflicts` rows are never pruned

`pruneMutations` (`sync.service.ts:146-154`) covers only `sync_mutations`. Resolved conflicts,
each with up to about 2.5 kB of `local` jsonb, stay until the transaction or the user is
hard-deleted. The volume is tiny. If you want to bound it, add the following to the same daily
job. It is a sequential scan of a small table.

```sql
DELETE FROM sync_conflicts WHERE status = 'resolved' AND resolved_at < now() - interval '180 days';
```

#### H5. The review list is unbounded

`ImportsService.review` (`imports.service.ts:281-325`) returns every pending row of the actor.
Its candidate query covers the full date span of those rows.

- **Measured.** 970 pending rows in a workspace of 50,000 rows took 6.4 ms. The plan is a bitmap
  scan on `transactions_created_by_idx` that filters out 49,030 rows. A five-year candidate span
  of 34,908 rows took 16 ms.
- **Growth.** Both grow linearly with pending rows (up to 5,000 per import, and imports
  accumulate), and the JSON response grows too.
- **When to act.** If large backlogs become real, page the list, for example at 500 rows, the
  confirm maximum. Optionally add
  `CREATE INDEX transactions_pending_idx ON transactions (created_by) WHERE review_status = 'pending_review' AND deleted_at IS NULL;`.
  Not needed today.

| Area                       | Evidence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Transfer CHECK             | `(transfer_id is null and not transfer_outside) or (transfer_id is not null and category_id is null)`. | Transfer validation paths  | All four writers check peers under the ledger lock. `confirm` refuses family scope or a category on a transfer (`schemas.ts:97-100`), a counterpart that is unconfirmed and does not point back, a used counterpart, a counterpart on the same account, and one in another workspace. | Ledger lock coverage       | Every write to `accounts`, `categories`, `transactions` and `sync_tombstones`, including FK `SET NULL` cascades, runs inside `ledgerTransaction`. The writes are serialized, so sync push, imports, family purge and account deletion cannot interleave.                                                     |
| Lock ordering              | The advisory lock is always taken first. Workspace row locks (`FOR NO KEY UPDATE` and `FOR KEY SHARE`) are compatible with FK checks. An accept that runs concurrently with a family delete cannot deadlock.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Version trigger            | Since 0011, `transaction_version` covers every field in `sameIntent` (`transfer_id` and `transfer_outside` included) plus `review_status` and `deleted_at`. | Timeouts                   | `statement_timeout` 30 s, `lock_timeout` 10 s (verified on the advisory lock), idle-in-transaction 60 s, and a 10 s connect timeout. Lock holders are an import of 5,000 rows at about 130 ms, plus suggestions at 197 ms and the candidate query at 16 ms. Purge takes 1.4 s at 50,000 rows.                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Indexes vs queries         | Transfer peers use `transactions_transfer_idx`, 0.06 ms. The candidate window uses `transactions_history_idx`, 0.45 ms for one month and 16 ms for five years. | N+1 queries                | Import makes one rate query per currency and chunked inserts of 500. Confirm is one bulk `UPDATE ... FROM (VALUES)`. Undo, purge and backfill are set-based. `suggestTransfers` is bucketed by day, not O(n x m). Push makes about 11 statements per mutation, which the previous audit accepted.                                                                                                                                                                             |

It is forward-only, as documented.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

The daily dump takes ACCESS SHARE only, and the app takes no table locks.                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Account deletion           | Runs in one ledger transaction. The cascades and `SET NULL` stamp the family rows, so devices see "former member" and hidden accounts.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

## Recommendations

### Immediate

- L1. Limit the last statement of the family purge to the family's own transfer ids.
- L3. Load rates for the pending rows' own currencies in `review()`.

### Short Term

- L2. Take the author name from the members list on the client.
- L4. Add the enum note to `docs/operations.md`.
- H2 and H3 together in one small migration, with FK `RESTRICT` and two CHECKs.

### Long Term

- H1. A deferred constraint trigger for the shape of a transfer, if direct SQL fixes become
  routine.
- H4 and H5. Prune resolved conflicts, and page the review list if pending backlogs grow.

## Overall Assessment

The database layer is in good shape. All ten findings of the previous audit are fixed or
correctly handed to the owner. The transfer feature fits the existing design:

- one CHECK for the category rule;
- app-side shape checks, made race-free by the single ledger lock;
- version bumps that include the new columns;
- an index for every new lookup.

The only finding with an effect on other tenants is L1. It is narrow. It needs a half-linked
transfer moved into another workspace, and it only turns a broken pair into a one-sided one, but
it should not touch other households at all. L3 is a small functional miss in BR-38's review
path.
